# Socratic Questioning

Question bank for Phase 2. Pick the few questions that fit what the user just said; don't work through the lists.

## Question categories

### Clarification: make sure you understand the idea

- What do you mean by [term]?
- Could you give a concrete example?
- What would this look like in practice, for one real user or one real day?
- How does this relate to [other thing they mentioned]?

### Scope: find the edges

- What is explicitly out of scope?
- What's the smallest version of this that would still help?
- Who is this for, and who is it not for?
- What would make you say "that's a different project"?

### Assumptions: surface what must be true

- What are we taking for granted here?
- What must be true for this to work?
- What would happen if [assumption] turned out to be wrong?
- Is that always the case, or are there exceptions?

### Alternatives: look sideways

- What other ways of solving this have you considered?
- What's the opposite approach, and why not that?
- What would you do if [main constraint] disappeared?
- How would [someone in another field] approach this?

### Consequences: look ahead

- What changes if this succeeds? Who notices first?
- What happens if it fails, or half-works?
- What else has to change if we do this (second-order effects)?
- How does this affect [related system or team]?

### Evidence: ground the claims

- What makes you confident this is needed?
- How could we test this cheaply before building it?
- Where has something like this worked or failed before?
- What would change your mind?

### Meta: question the question

- Is this the right problem to solve, or a symptom of another one?
- What are we optimising for?
- Is now the right time?

## Pacing

- **Early rounds:** clarification and scope. You can't analyse what you don't understand.
- **Middle rounds:** assumptions, alternatives and consequences.
- **Later rounds:** evidence and meta questions, and anything a research finding has raised.

Two to four questions per round. One sharp question beats four generic ones.

## Pitfalls

- **Leading questions.** "Wouldn't it be better to…?" steers the user to your answer. Ask open questions.
- **Judging too early.** Criticism belongs in Phase 4; during clarification it makes people defensive.
- **Re-asking.** If the conversation or an earlier brainstorm file already answers it, build on that answer instead.
- **Interrogating.** Acknowledge what you learned before asking more, and offer to move on when the picture is clear.
