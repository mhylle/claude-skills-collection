# Analysis Frameworks

How to run the three Phase 4 frameworks. Use the one or two that Phase 4's table picks for the idea type, and within a framework keep only the parts that produce insight for this idea.

## Six Thinking Hats

Look at the idea from six deliberate angles, one at a time, so risks, benefits and feelings don't blur together.

| Hat | Focus | Prompts |
|---|---|---|
| White | Facts | What do we know? What have we observed versus assumed? What data is missing, and what would it take to get it? |
| Red | Intuition | What's the gut reaction? What feels risky, or exciting? How will users feel about it? |
| Black | Risks | What could go wrong? What obstacles, weak points, compliance or timing problems exist? What resources might we lack? |
| Yellow | Benefits | What value does this create, and for whom? What's the best realistic outcome? What problems does it solve? |
| Green | Alternatives | What unconventional approach exists? What if we combined it with something else, or removed a constraint? What analogies from other fields apply? |
| Blue | Process | Is this the right problem? What are the decision criteria? What's the next step, and who needs to be involved? |

Run the hats that carry weight for this idea; three or four is often enough. The Black hat feeds the Risks table in the output, the Yellow hat the Strengths, the Green hat the alternatives worth keeping.

## SCAMPER

Seven prompts for changing an idea systematically. Most useful for product features, processes and open ideas.

| Letter | Question | Prompts |
|---|---|---|
| Substitute | What could be replaced with something better? | another component, technology, process, person, place or time |
| Combine | What could be merged? | features, systems, steps or purposes that could become one |
| Adapt | What could be borrowed? | patterns from another domain, how others solved it, a variant for different users |
| Modify | What could be amplified or reduced? | bigger or smaller, faster or slower, simpler, more or less frequent |
| Put to other use | What else could it do? | other users, other problems, an unexpected context |
| Eliminate | What could go? | unnecessary features, steps or complexity; what if we did nothing? |
| Reverse | What could be reordered or inverted? | the opposite order, swapped roles, working backwards |

Keep only the letters that produce something useful; list those findings under Enhancement Opportunities.

## Premortem

Imagine the idea has already failed, then work backwards to causes you can prevent now. Best for risky, expensive or hard-to-reverse ideas.

1. **Set the scene:** "It's six months from now and this has failed. What went wrong?"
2. **Collect failure modes** from several angles:
   - Technical: performance, security, integration, data
   - People: skills, ownership, adoption, resistance to change
   - Process and scope: unclear requirements, scope creep, wrong priorities
   - External: market, competitors, regulation, technology shifts
3. **Ask what was ignored or underestimated:** warning signs, dismissed feedback, complexity, time, dependencies, and assumptions that proved wrong.
4. **Keep the three to five most realistic failure modes.** For each, record the failure, its warning signs, how to prevent it, and the fallback if it happens anyway.

These go under Premortem Findings in the output, and the main ones also go into the Risks table.
